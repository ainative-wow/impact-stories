01Engineering impact story

More delivery.Less overhead.Stronger ownership.

The new way of working increased delivery velocity while reducing team size and handoffs — without a noticeable dip in delivery speed. This story walks through how we work differently, what changed, the numbers behind it and what is still open.

    More delivery

    Tickets were often completed ahead of schedule, and there was no noticeable dip in delivery speed.

    Less overhead

    The squad went from 11 to ~7 people, with fewer handoffs between frontend and backend work.

    Stronger ownership

    Frontend and backend work can be combined in one ticket and carried end to end.

    02How we work differently

    What changed

    The change is as much about how work is cut as about who does it: smaller team, broader ownership and a ticket that carries business context straight into build.

      Smaller team

      The squad reduced from 11 to ~7 people while delivery stayed steady.

      Broader ownership

      Frontend and backend work could be combined into a single ticket and owned end to end.

      Fewer handoffs

      Fewer handoffs and dependencies between steps, so work flows faster.

      04Closing the gap before code

      Better business alignment

      The team now pulls business context earlier and more deeply, from Epic to Ticket level, reducing gaps before development starts.

      Earlier in the lifecycleCloser to development

      Before

      Business context appeared immediately before development, after the ticket was already defined.

      Now

      Context is pulled forward and enters between Epic and Ticket, so the ticket starts with it.

      Effect

      Fewer gaps are left to close once development starts.

      05What still needs solving

      The tension we haven't solved yet

      Pulling additional tickets forward does not fit neatly into traditional sprint reporting.

      Pull work forward

      • Better flow
      • Fewer idle periods
      • More completed work
      • Early finishes become productive

      Protect sprint predictability

      • Late blocker
      • Sprint commitment changes
      • Reporting becomes harder to interpret
      • Next ticket may not be sufficiently refined
      Early finish = good

      Finishing early frees capacity that would otherwise sit idle.

      Committing to pull ahead = reporting risk

      Open questionHow do we preserve the benefits of pulling work forward without distorting sprint commitments and reporting?
      06The takeaway

      The team is delivering faster with a leaner, more end-to-end operating model.

      Smaller, well-scoped work moves through review and merge quickly. Fewer handoffs and broader ownership keep it moving, and delivery stays steady.

      1. Smaller workWell-scoped items
      2. Faster flowMedian merge in 0.6 hours
      3. Fewer handoffsFewer dependencies
      4. More ownershipFull-stack tickets
      5. Steady deliveryNo noticeable dip

      What we can say

      Delivery speed held, tickets often finished early, 53 PRs merged in about nine weeks, and the squad is smaller.

      What we don't claim

      No percentage gains in productivity, quality or speed, and no claim that fewer people caused the results.

      What is open

      Pulling work forward and sprint reporting still pull in different directions.

      Next challenge: make pull-ahead work visible without compromising sprint transparency.
